Conversational AI Quality

AI Quality Engineering for Conversational AI Avatars

Principal Software Quality Engineer · AI-enabled conversational simulation platform

Focus: Conversational AI, AI evaluation, hallucination testing, prompt validation, transcription quality, edge case testing and end to end AI quality.

Challenge
The platform evolved from a conventional meeting and course workflow into an AI avatar based conversational experience. The new architecture introduced AI generated responses, conversational prompts, scoring, transcription and dynamic user interactions. Traditional functional testing was no longer sufficient. Quality had to be validated across the AI response, conversational context, behavioural boundaries, transcription, scoring and supporting data.
Impact

Reported outcome

  • Expanded QA coverage from conventional functional testing to AI behaviour and failure condition testing
  • Exposed AI defects that were difficult to reproduce through normal user flows
  • Helped establish stronger behavioural boundaries for conversational AI
  • Prevented empty responses from progressing into downstream scoring
  • Connected upstream transcription quality with downstream AI evaluation and scoring
  • Improved confidence in AI generated outcomes through frontend, backend and log level validation
Key learning
“AI quality is not only about whether the model produces a correct answer. It also requires testing whether the model stays within its assigned context, role and behavioural boundaries, and whether the surrounding system correctly captures, validates and evaluates its output.”
What I did
I designed and executed exploratory and edge case driven testing across the AI interaction lifecycle. Key areas included:
  • Context and response validation
  • Hallucination and behavioural boundary testing
  • Role reversal detection
  • Response accuracy and grounding
  • Prompt and instruction validation
  • Transcription quality and downstream scoring
  • AI response latency and workflow behaviour
  • Database level validation using DynamoDB
  • Log based investigation using Sentry and AWS CloudWatch
  • Frontend to backend correlation for AI generated outcomes
Network loss
One significant edge case involved temporary internet loss during an avatar interaction. The user's captured response became empty while the Continue action remained available, allowing an empty response to proceed into the scoring workflow. I reproduced the issue by deliberately interrupting connectivity during an active interaction, correlated the transcription and workflow state through logs, and recommended validation before allowing the interaction to continue. The development team implemented the validation.
Role reversal
I also identified a role reversal behaviour where repeatedly returning the avatar's question caused the AI to move outside its intended role and begin answering as the user. The issue was investigated at the prompt and background instruction level and the relevant instructions were subsequently updated.
Transcription
Another investigation identified transcription loss during avatar communication. I qualified the discrepancy, escalated it to the relevant team and supported isolated component testing. The team subsequently updated the transcription library, after which the transcription no longer lost parts of the user's spoken communication.

Data Integrity · Workflow Quality

Data Integrity and Complex Workflow Quality Engineering

Principal Software Quality Engineer · AI-enabled conversational simulation platform

Focus: Data integrity, database validation, migration testing, complex associations, reporting accuracy, state management and product quality.

Challenge
The platform contained interconnected relationships between companies, users, courses, scenarios, assignments, assessments and reporting data. Several major features changed these relationships, including cross company migration and a reporting dashboard covering Practice and Certification activity. The main quality risk was not simply whether a feature worked on the screen. A change to one relationship could affect user state, course visibility, assessment history, scores and reporting results.
Impact

Reported outcome

  • Increased confidence in complex business reporting by validating the data behind the displayed numbers
  • Exposed database and association defects that were not visible through UI validation alone
  • Protected user state, scores and assessment history during cross company migration
  • Validated forward and reverse migration paths
  • Strengthened end to end coverage across UI, database, administration and reporting layers
  • Reduced the risk of incorrect assignment, certification and proficiency information reaching users
Key learning
  • “Quality in data driven systems depends on validating relationships, state and business calculations, not just individual screens or records.”
  • “A successful migration means more than moving data. It means proving that the relationships, history and user state still mean the same thing after the move.”
What I did
I approached these features from both the user and data perspectives.
Migration
For cross company migration, I performed deep end to end validation of forward and reverse movement between companies. I verified:
  • Correct company and user associations
  • Course visibility and ownership
  • Scenario relationships
  • Completed, in progress and untouched states
  • Scores and assessment history
  • Forward and reverse migration
  • Admin and reporting data
  • Underlying database relationships
Rather than treating migration as a simple record movement exercise, I validated whether the complete user state and historical information remained meaningful after the move.
Reporting
For the reporting dashboard, I created a controlled non production dataset with known expected outcomes rather than relying only on existing records. I used SQL queries and scripts to validate the underlying data and calculations across Practice and Certification. The validation covered assignment status, users, attempts, progress, proficiency, certification outcomes and company or team level reporting. This approach exposed discrepancies and calculation defects that were subsequently addressed by the team.
